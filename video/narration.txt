This is GardenOS — a single web page that grows an entire garden, through an entire year.
It began as an abstract question: what does a plant mean, when it stops being a flower, and becomes light?
That question turned into something specific — a real London cottage garden, rebuilt from the ground up, one note at a time.
Watch the year turn. In spring, bulbs push up through cold earth — snowdrops, then daffodils — and the apple and cherry trees break into blossom on bare branches.
Summer fills the borders. Foxgloves and alliums, hydrangeas and geraniums. Bees and butterflies work the flowers, and birds come down to the bath.
Every plant keeps its own clock. So when autumn comes, each tree turns in its own time, and to its own colour — gold, then russet, then scarlet — while the apples ripen, redden, and fall to the grass below.
Winter strips it back — to bare branches, evergreen structure, and the very first snowdrops, beginning again.
And as the light fades, the lights along the patio flicker on.
One page. One year. Built frame by frame, in conversation.
